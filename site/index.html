Learn guitar parts by ear.

Rusty Riffs is a desktop practice player. Slow a recording down without changing its pitch, shift it for songs in other tunings, loop the hard bits seamlessly and mark the sections you're working on.

Everything you need to practise, and no more

Slow down, same pitch

Play anything from 10% to 150% speed. The notes stay where they are, so you can hear every one of them.

Shift the pitch

±12 semitones with ±50 cents of fine tuning, independent of speed. Play along in standard tuning or match a slightly detuned record.

Seamless loops

Save named loops from your markers. Each remembers its own speed, and an optional pre-roll gives you a beat to get ready.

Speed trainer

Every pass of the loop goes a few percent faster, until you're playing it at full speed.

Markers that land on the note

Drop a marker while listening. The app compensates for audio latency and your reaction time, and can snap to the nearest note attack.

Remembers every song

Markers, loops and settings are saved per song, keyed by the file's contents, so they come back even if you rename or move it.

Keep your left hand on the neck

Nearly every shortcut sits under the right hand, vim-style, so you can drive the app between phrases without putting the guitar down. Press ? in the app for the full list.

Looking up the latest release…

Linux

    Make the AppImage executable with chmod +x, then run it. Audio goes through ALSA, which reaches PipeWire and PulseAudio.

    Windows

      The installer isn't code-signed, so SmartScreen warns you. Click More info, then Run anyway.

      macOS

        Apple Silicon only. The app isn't signed: after moving it to Applications, run xattr -cr "/Applications/Rusty Riffs.app" once, then open it normally.

        Prefer to build it yourself? The README has the steps for every OS. Supported formats: mp3, wav, flac, m4a (AAC) and ogg (Vorbis).
